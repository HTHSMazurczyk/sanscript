Lesson 1 - Letters

Welcome to lesson one! Before we start, I wanted to give you a little overview of what you are going to expect not only in this lesson, but in future lessons as well. This beginning paragraph, or these beginning paragraphs, will be seen in future lessons as you continue and will explain the basics of what you are going to learn in the full lesson. Then you get into the actual lesson, where you learn what the lesson you chose covers, such as this lesson covering the letters and the sounds they make. Understood? Ok, let's start!

Lesson one, as I just said, is about the letters you will encounter. But before we get to that, I need to share a bit more information to make things easier. The letters that you see later on will look like English characters, but are not pronounced like English characters. This is the Romanized version of Sanskrit, or the International Alphabet of Sanskrit Transliteration (IAST). It basically makes the letters into English ones. You don’t need to know a whole lot about IAST, as it will be used a lot here.

Below, you are going to find a table. This contains the consonants in Sanskrit. Listen to how each letter is pronounced and replicate it. Make sure you fully understand how you say each letter before moving on to the next consonants. When you are ready, you can continue to the vowels, where we can really get into making sounds. Please note that all of the consonants are pronounced with an “a” vowel at the end, as trying to pronounce just the consonants without the vowel is actual torture. Just remember that as you listen in.

These vowels are going to be pretty important to know for the next lesson on syllables. The consonants are also going to be equally important in that regard, specifically the ones represented by two letters. These two-lettered consonants are considered one grouping, so they can never be split into the individual letters that make them up.

The final part is codas. These are just unique sounds for Sanskrit and come with their own weird pronunciation. These are ending sounds for words and will have a vowel before them. So, similar to the consonants, these codas are pronounced with a leading “a” sound. However, you can substitute any vowel before the coda for further practice.
