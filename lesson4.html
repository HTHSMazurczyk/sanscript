Lesson 4 - Sentences and Songs

Welcome to lesson 4! Hope you did a lot of practice with the last lesson because you’re going to need it here. As you saw in the title, we are going to be pronouncing entire sentences. This isn’t really any new content, mostly just an extension of your abilities, so this lesson will be fairly short with a little bit of new information. So, let’s get started!


Sentences are not very different from words; it’s just that you have to string multiple of them together. Let’s start with an example sentence just to get us into things. Don’t listen to the audio yet; you can listen to it later to check your accuracy:

If you listened to the sentence audio, you would have been able to pick up on the slight melody that was put on the word. That is what creates the song part of Sanskrit and was created to make spoken Sanskrit easier. Sorry if you were struggling to pronounce the entire sentence up there, but that was to get you comfortable with being able to pronounce sentences. Now, to make it easier.


Sentences in Sanskrit can usually be divided into individual syllables and split up by syllables. To make a good-sounding song and to have a better time with pronunciation, expand all of the words in the line into their syllables. Then, split the line in half by the syllables and round to the nearest word. That might sound confusing, but let’s use an example. Take the sentence below and work through it with me.

Guruśukraśanibhyaśca rāhave ketave namaḥ

First lets break it down into syllables



Gu - ru - śuk - ra - śa - nibh - yaś - ca rā - ha - ve ke - ta - ve na - maḥ

Once we have this, we can split it in half and rejoin the words back together. The rounding part just means that we can’t put the halfway point inside a word. Now that we've done that, we can pronounce each half of the line separately, leaving a small gap between the halves. This gap not only provides auditory comfort, but also allows the speaker to take a short break and pronounce the rest of the line instead of slowly going syllable by syllable.


Now that you have two halves, pronounce them one by one using a rhythm. It could be your own rhythm or one that you copied from my usage before, but using a rhythm will make speaking Sanskrit much easier than doing it all flat. One interpretation you could do is this. To actually check yourself, I recommend just looking at the point where I take a break to test your ability to split the sentence in two. Then, you can compare pronunciations and make sure they line up, but I have confidence in you that you can do that correctly.
